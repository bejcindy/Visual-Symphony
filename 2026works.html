The Azure Sanctuary/Voyage to the Moon

Music by Heron Zhao, illustrated by Yijia Sun

Sun Yijia is a professional illustrator who graduated from the Illustration Department at Ringling College of Art and Design. The two artworks presented in this exhibition were created in collaboration with a friend as visual backgrounds for her music video. The first depicts a dreamlike blue forest with a hidden shrine and small cat spirits, while the second features a majestic waterfall beneath a bright full moon, accompanied by a flying fish boat.

Through deep blue and teal tones, Sun creates a tranquil, atmospheric world that complements the emotions of the music. Her artistic practice focuses on using color and lighting to construct narrative spaces, often incorporating small creatures to bring life to her environments. Through this collaboration, she hopes to offer audiences an immersive audiovisual experience that highlights the connection between visual art and music.

Omen of the Wheel/Bird in a Cage

Music by Monstar Wanying Cao, illustrated by Ruohan Huang

Program Note from Monstar:

Inspired by a mysterious, silver-haired figure overlooking a clock-like structure, Omen of the Wheel explores the passage of time, the uncertainty of fate, and the presence of an unknown force. The music begins with an ethereal and mysterious atmosphere, gradually unfolding into something eerie and unsettling before culminating in an epic finale. Through this transformation, the piece evokes a world where time, destiny, and the supernatural intertwine.

Program Note from Ruohan:

While the owner is away, the cage is accidentally knocked over by a cat. The little bird inside has no way to save itself and can only let death and despair inch closer. In this commission, I explored the backstory of the protagonist—the girl in the image—and chose the last scene of her life. The dark red carpet and pale fabric set a tone that evokes death. The bars of the cage and the beaded chain create visual leading lines, while the disc-like cage structure makes her seem like she’s on Death’s plate, powerless against her inevitable fate. I hope these choices, while staying true to the character, deliver a strong visual and emotional impact—and also prompt some reflection on irresponsible pet ownership.

Artist: Ruohan Huang

Ruohan Huang is a freelance illustrator and a graduate of Ringling College of Art and Design. When working on commissions, her favorite part is exploring the protagonist’s backstory, identifying a meaningful moment, and capturing it through a distinctive visual language.

For this exhibition, she selected one of her favorite commissioned pieces and, with the client’s permission, collaborated with a producer friend to present it in a new audiovisual format. She hopes the final result will allow audiences to experience the unique beauty that emerges when visual art and sound come together.

Composer: Monstar Wanying Cao

Monstar Wanying Cao is an award-winning composer, pianist, and media artist, currently a Ph.D. candidate in Digital Composition at the University of California, Riverside. Her creative work explores film scoring, audiovisual storytelling, and the intersection of music and emerging technologies. She is the co-founder and curator of Visual Symphony, an international interdisciplinary platform dedicated to fostering creative collaborations between composers, filmmakers, and visual artists. Her artistic practice bridges contemporary composition, multimedia production, and cross-disciplinary experimentation.

Pigeons Invade the City

AI-generated image series, Created using Gemini by Ziwei Tang, Music by Yunqi Li

Program Notes from Ziwei:

Pigeon Invasion originates in the artist’s observations of urban pigeons during her time in the UK. Created using Gemini image-generation, the series introduces pigeons into familiar settings, including UCLH, IKEA, GAIL’s, and the London Overground, transforming everyday environments into sites of absurd interspecies encounters. Although pigeons inhabit the city extensively, their presence remains subject to human conventions of hygiene, function, and acceptable behaviour. As they enter spaces organised around healthcare, consumption, and transit, the usually unremarked allocation of space becomes visible. Moving between humour and unease, the work examines the human-centred order of urban life, turning “invasion” itself into a question: who is entitled to claim a space as their everyday world, and to define another species’ presence as a transgression?

Program Notes from Yunqi:

Pigeons Invade the City is a playful musical portrayal of an unexpected takeover of the urban landscape. Through lively rhythms, energetic melodies, and humorous musical gestures, the piece captures the chaotic yet charming movements of pigeons as they flutter, wander, and gather throughout the city. What begins as a few scattered birds gradually becomes a lively invasion, transforming familiar streets and rooftops into their playground. Beneath its humorous character, the piece invites listeners to reconsider the relationship between humans and nature, reminding us that cities are shared spaces filled with life, movement, and unexpected surprises.

Artist: Ziwei Tang

Ziwei holds a BA (Hons) in Fine Art from Central Saint Martins, University of the Arts London, and is currently based in Beijing. Working across artistic practice, independent research, and writing, her work engages with posthumanist discourse, exploring the possibility of moving beyond the anthropocentric frameworks of understanding consciousness, and approaching the perceptual modes and experiential worlds of the non-human.

Her work has been shortlisted for the Royal Academy of Arts Summer Exhibition. She is the co-founder and co-curator of Visual Symphony, an interdisciplinary art project bringing together visual artists, moving-image creators, and music composers, with exhibitions presented in London and California.

Composer: Yunqi Li

Yunqi Li is a contemporary Chinese composer and a Doctorate of Musical Arts (D.M.A.) candidate who was selected as a Houston Composer Fellow for the Texas New Music Festival (TNMF). Her work often bridges the gap between acoustic composition, music education, and electronic music.

Counting Blades

Film by Micaela Randolph ; Music by Xiaowei Cao

In regards to the film itself, it is titled "Counting blades", and is a mixed-media stop motion video inspired by the idea of "counting sheep". Serving as an ode to my sleep paralysis, the piece replicates my perspective before I try to sleep. I count how many times my fan spins but instead of falling into a deep sleep, I am met with hallucinations conjured up by my subconscious. It currently has no sound to accompany it, so I would love to see someone else' interpretation in that realm.

Dream repeats in nebulous ways; reality repeats in nebulous ways.

Artist: Micaela Randolph

Micaela Randolph is a TFDP student at University of California, Riverside.

Composer: Xiaowei Cao

Xiaowei Cao (b. 1999) is an artist, currently getting his Ph.D. in Music Composition and Technology at the University of Florida. He works with repetition, texts, and products of the internet culture.

Pune Metamorphosis: Shiva’s Resonance (2024)

Work by Paulo C. Chagas

Ambisonics audiovisual composition

Pune Metamorphosis: Shiva’s Resonance explores the interplay between sacred and secular sound worlds within the urban landscape of Pune, India, focusing on sonic environments surrounding two Shiva temples on the city’s outskirts. The work integrates ambisonic soundscapes and 3D video recordings made in 2019, creating an immersive audiovisual experience.

The piece unfolds as a multidimensional soundscape in which human activity, animal life, environmental sound, and urban technologies coexist within an evolving spatial field shaped by architecture and temporal flow. A secondary sonic layer uses granular synthesis to transform the original recordings, foregrounding non-conscious processes of sound perception and interactions between human and non-human agencies.

The visual component engages directly with the recorded locations, highlighting spatial movement, technical mediation, and human presence. The work is presented in binaural format and remains adaptable to ambisonic performance.

Artist: Paulo C. Chagas

Paulo C. Chagas is an internationally recognized composer and Distinguished Professor of Composition at the University of California, Riverside. His catalogue includes over 230 works spanning orchestral, chamber, electroacoustic, and audiovisual media. His compositions explore the intersections of sound, space, technology, and human experience, often integrating electronics, spatial audio, and artificial intelligence. He holds a PhD in Musicology from the Université de Liège and previously served as sound director and composer-in-residence at the WDR Electronic Studio in Cologne. His works have been performed internationally, and he serves on the board of the International Computer Music Association (ICMA).

Call Me When You Grow Up

Directed by Cartee Wang，Music by Monstar Cao

I came up with this story while thinking about growing up, memories, and the people we leave behind. Sometimes, when we move to a new place, we find things that remind us of people we haven’t seen or talked to in a long time. I wanted to tell a simple story with a little mystery and emotion.

Call Me When You Grow Up follows May, a young girl who has just moved into a new home. While unpacking her things, she finds an old photograph of herself with another young girl. On the back of the photo, she discovers a phone number. She starts writing a message but hesitates before sending it. Suddenly, her phone rings, and the number is the same one written on the photograph.

Through this film, I wanted to explore memories, growing up, and how the past can unexpectedly become part of our present.

Director: Cartee Wang

Cartee Wang is an international student from Qingdao, China, currently in her senior year studying Theatre, Film, and Digital Production at the University of California, Riverside. She is passionate about acting, filmmaking, and storytelling. Through her work in film production and performance, she continues to explore different approaches to cinema. She hopes to create films with a sense of beauty, harmony, and compelling narratives.

Fortune House

Directed by Exa Huang, Music by Jiawei Shen and Tim Juliano

In the late nineteenth century, as the first waves of Asian immigrants arrived in North America, newspapers and political cartoons often portrayed them as grotesque monsters. Turning a community into something feared and inhuman made exclusion easier to justify; before people could be removed, they first had to be dehumanized.

Inspired by these historical images, Fortune House reimagines the immigrant experience as a contemporary fable. Set in a near-future Los Angeles, the film follows an undocumented Chinese delivery driver who stumbles into a restaurant run by demons and is forced to gamble for his place in America through a supernatural game of mahjong. Blending magical realism with dark humor, the film transforms the anxieties, compromises, and hidden transactions of immigrant survival into a Faustian bargain, asking a single question: when the powerless are forced to negotiate with a system rigged against them, what do they have left to put on the table?

Director: Exa Huang

Exa Huang is a Los Angeles–based director and producer whose work combines intimate realism with contemporary genre sensibilities to explore underground subcultures, immigrant identities, and marginalized communities。 An American Film Institute Conservatory graduate, Huang previously worked as a director and creative at VICE and in development and acquisitions at Valhalla Entertainment. Her narrative short I HATE LOVE received support from The Chimaera Project. Her documentary short JUST ANOTHER DAY was recognized by the Montreal Independent Film Festival and Independent Shorts Awards, while STRANGER IN PARADISE screened at queer film festivals in Berlin, Shanghai, and London.

Her commercial and editorial work includes commissions and features for GQ, Burberry, Nike, NOWNESS, The Face, Marshall, and Shanghai Fashion Week. She is currently developing narrative projects centered on diaspora, labor, and contemporary Asian underground culture.

Ashes

Directed by Yuan Cao, Music by Monstar Wanying Cao

The film is a psychological drama that examines the complexities of motherhood, mental health and individual responsibility. It challenges the idealized image of the selfless mother and portrays a woman who is pushed towards desperation by postpartum depression, familial oppression and economic hardship. Rather than having a single villain, it explores how individual choices blurs the boundaries between guilt, responsibility and victimhood.

Furthermore, the film is also about the power of speaking out. The protagonist’s journey from being questioned and judged to reclaiming her voice becomes an act of resistance against the horrible fate that’s imposed upon her.

We do not seek to offer easy answers or moral judgments. Instead, I hope to create an intimate and unsettling experience that invites the audiences to look beyond the label of “mother”, confront the human suffering hidden beneath it and question the system that allows such suffering to persist. Ultimately, we want her story to be not simply understood, but impossible to ignore

Director: Yuan Cao

Yuan Cao is a Chinese director/filmmaker currently studying for his MFA Filmmaking degree at New York Film Academy in Los Angeles. Drawn to psychological drama and suspense, he is interested in stories that explore the complexities of human nature, interpersonal relationships and moral ambiguity. His work is usually driven by characters with contradictory personalities, and often explores the emotional and psychological forces behind seemingly irreversible choices.
